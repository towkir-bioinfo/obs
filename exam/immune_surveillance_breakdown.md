# Immune Surveillance of Cancer

## Immunogenicity of Chemically Induced Tumors

### Initial Setup

- A mouse is injected with a chemical carcinogen to induce a tumor.
- The tumor is surgically removed (excised) and cultured to create a live tumor cell line.

![Initial setup: inject carcinogen, tumor forms, excise and culture](images/step1_initial_setup.png)

### Path A: The Vaccinated Group

- **Action:** The cultured tumor cells are irradiated (killed) so they can no longer divide, then injected into a healthy, naive mouse as a "vaccine."
- **Challenge:** This vaccinated mouse is later challenged with live tumor cells.
- **Result:** The mouse's immune system recognizes the antigens and destroys the cancer cells, leaving the mouse tumor-free.

![Vaccinated path: irradiate cells, vaccinate, challenge, remains tumor free](images/step2_vaccinated_path.png)

### Path B: The Unvaccinated Control

- **Action:** A fresh, naive mouse receives live tumor cells directly, without any prior vaccination.
- **Result:** Because its immune system has no memory of these antigens, the immune response is too slow, and the mouse develops a tumor.

![Control path: live cells, challenge naive mouse, develops tumor](images/step3_control_path.png)

### Takeaway

> Mice immunized with tumor cells are protected against subsequent tumor growth.
