
# PCR Reagents, Their Roles, and Precautions

A standard PCR reaction (typically 20–25 µL) requires the following core components:

---

## 1. Template DNA

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Contains the target sequence to be amplified |
| **Typical amount** | 50–100 ng genomic DNA per reaction |
| **Precautions** | • Keep on ice • Avoid repeated freeze-thaw cycles • Poor 260/280 (<1.8) or 260/230 (<1.8) may inhibit PCR • Do not overload — excess DNA inhibits amplification |

---

## 2. Forward Primer

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Binds to the antisense strand; defines the 5′ start of the amplicon |
| **Typical concentration** | 0.1–0.5 µM final (usually 0.2 µM) |
| **Precautions** | • Store at −20°C as concentrated stock (100 µM) • Avoid freeze-thaw; aliquot • Check for self-dimers, hairpins, and cross-dimers • GC content 40–60% ideal |

---

## 3. Reverse Primer

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Binds to the sense strand; defines the 3′ end of the amplicon |
| **Typical concentration** | 0.1–0.5 µM final (usually 0.2 µM) |
| **Precautions** | • Same as forward primer • Tm should be within 2–3°C of forward primer • Avoid 3′ complementarity between primer pairs |

---

## 4. dNTPs (Deoxynucleoside Triphosphates)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Building blocks (A, T, G, C) incorporated into the new DNA strand by Taq polymerase |
| **Typical concentration** | 200 µM each dNTP (800 µM total) |
| **Precautions** | • Store at −20°C • Avoid repeated freeze-thaw • Excess dNTPs can chelate Mg²⁺ and inhibit PCR • Use molecular biology grade |

---

## 5. Taq DNA Polymerase

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Enzyme that synthesizes new DNA strands by adding dNTPs to the primer 3′-OH end |
| **Typical amount** | 1–2.5 U per 25 µL reaction |
| **Precautions** | • Keep at −20°C; never leave at room temp • Add last to the master mix • Avoid vortexing — mix gently • Excess enzyme causes non-specific amplification • Use hot-start versions to reduce non-specific binding |

---

## 6. MgCl₂ (Magnesium Chloride)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Cofactor for Taq polymerase; neutralizes negative charge of dNTPs and DNA backbone |
| **Typical concentration** | 1.5–2.5 mM final (often supplied with buffer) |
| **Precautions** | • Too low → enzyme inactive • Too high → non-specific bands, primer dimers • May need optimization per primer pair • Often provided as part of 10× buffer |

---

## 7. 10× PCR Buffer

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Maintains optimal pH and ionic strength for enzyme activity |
| **Typical concentration** | 1× final |
| **Precautions** | • Contains Tris-HCl, KCl, MgCl₂ (sometimes), and stabilizers • Vortex before use if precipitate forms • Use the buffer supplied with your specific polymerase • Some buffers include DMSO or BSA for GC-rich templates |

---

## 8. Nuclease-Free Water (NF Water)

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Role** | Makes up the final volume; dilutes all components |
| **Typical amount** | Variable — brings reaction to final volume |
| **Precautions** | • Use only nuclease-free, PCR-grade water • Never use regular distilled water • Keep sterile to avoid contamination |

---

## Optional Additives

| Reagent | Role | Precautions |
|---|---|---|
| **DMSO (1–10%)** | Improves GC-rich template amplification | Can reduce enzyme activity; use fresh |
| **Betaine (1–2 M)** | Enhances GC-rich amplification | May inhibit at high concentrations |
| **BSA (0.1–0.5 µg/µL)** | Stabilizes enzyme, reduces inhibition | Use acetylated BSA |
| **Formamide (1–5%)** | Helps denature GC-rich templates | Toxic; handle with care |

---

## Master Mix Preparation (Example: 25 µL Reaction)

| Component | Volume (µL) | Final Conc. |
|---|---|---|
| 10× PCR Buffer | 2.5 | 1× |
| MgCl₂ (25 mM) | 1.5 | 1.5 mM |
| dNTPs (2.5 mM each) | 2.0 | 200 µM each |
| Forward primer (10 µM) | 0.5 | 0.2 µM |
| Reverse primer (10 µM) | 0.5 | 0.2 µM |
| Taq polymerase (5 U/µL) | 0.2 | 1 U |
| Template DNA (50 ng/µL) | 1.0 | 50 ng |
| NF water | 16.8 | — |
| **Total** | **25.0** | |

---

## General Precautions for PCR

1. **Always work on ice** — prepare master mix on ice; add enzyme last.
2. **Use gloves** — prevent DNase/RNase contamination from skin.
3. **Use sterile filter tips** — prevent cross-contamination between samples.
4. **Prepare a master mix** — for multiple reactions, prepare a common mix to reduce pipetting error.
5. **Avoid freeze-thaw cycles** — aliquot primers, dNTPs, and enzyme.
6. **Keep separate areas** — pre-PCR (reagent prep) and post-PCR (analysis) areas should be physically separated.
7. **Use positive and negative controls** — positive: known template; negative: NF water instead of DNA.
8. **Centrifuge briefly** — before placing tubes in thermocycler to collect contents at bottom.
9. **Do not vortex enzyme** — mix by gentle pipetting.
10. **Check primer quality** — bad primers are a common cause of PCR failure.
11. **Optimize annealing temperature** — usually 5°C below primer Tm; use gradient PCR if needed.
12. **Avoid contamination** — use fresh aliquots of water and reagents; clean pipettes regularly.

---

## Quick Checklist Before PCR

- [ ] Template DNA quality checked (260/280 ~1.8, 260/230 ~2.0)
- [ ] Primers diluted to working concentration (10 µM)
- [ ] dNTPs aliquoted and not freeze-thawed repeatedly
- [ ] Taq polymerase stored at −20°C
- [ ] MgCl₂ concentration optimized
- [ ] Master mix prepared on ice
- [ ] Negative control included
- [ ] Thermocycler program set correctly
