# 4D Framework for AI Fluency

> [!definition]
> The **4D Framework for AI Fluency** is a model of the core competencies needed to
> work with AI systems **effectively, efficiently, ethically, and safely**. It breaks
> that ability into four interacting skill areas — **Delegation, Description,
> Discernment, and Diligence** — that together describe not just *how* to use AI tools
> but *when*, *why*, and *with what responsibility*.

## The four competencies

| D | Question it answers | What it involves |
|---|---|---|
| **Delegation** | *What should AI do, what should I do, and what should we do together?* | Problem awareness, platform awareness (knowing a tool's strengths/limits), and deciding how to divide a task between human and AI. |
| **Description** | *How do I tell the AI what I want?* | Communicating clearly with the system: describing the desired **product** (output), the **process** (how to get there), and the **performance** (role, tone, constraints) — i.e. skilled prompting. |
| **Discernment** | *Is what the AI produced any good?* | Critically evaluating the **product** (accuracy, quality), the **process** (was the reasoning sound?), and the **performance** (did it behave appropriately?). Catching errors, hallucinations, and bias. |
| **Diligence** | *Am I using AI responsibly?* | Transparency about AI use, thoughtful tool selection (**creation**), honest disclosure (**transparency**), and accountable release of AI-assisted work (**deployment**). |

## Key ideas

- The four D's are **mutually reinforcing**, not sequential — better description improves discernment, discernment feedback sharpens delegation, and diligence governs all of it.
- Fluency is about **judgement**, not memorised prompts: it transfers across models and tools as they change.
- Aim: interactions with AI that are **effective** (achieve the goal), **efficient** (worth the effort), **ethical** (aligned with values), and **safe** (risks understood and managed).
