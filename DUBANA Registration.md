## Background

Salinity is a growing constraint on rice production in coastal and irrigated areas, including Bangladesh and other South Asian countries (Dasgupta et al., 2017). O. sativa is a glycophyte and highly sensitive to salt stress (Hoang et al., 2016). Oryza coarctata is a halophyte of wild species of rice which thrives well under high saline as well as submerged conditions (Sarkar et al., 2021). It has outstanding salt tolerance up to 240 mM NaCl (Prusty et al., 2026). The halophytic wild rice species Oryza coarctata has a remarkable ability to resist salt stress and can complete its life cycle under relatively high salt concentrations (>400 mM or >40 dS/m), and even set a few grains per panicle (Ishikawa et al., 2022).

Being a glycophyte Oryza sativa lacks this salt tolerance but it is much more high yielding than Oryza coarctata. A main reason for this is the gene and their expression difference (Kumar et al., 2012). A key gene regulating the salt tolerance is the metallothionein (Kumar et al., 2012). The type 3 metallothionein shares an 82% sequence similarity (homology) with the corresponding OsMT-I-3a metallothionein of Oryza sativa (Usha et al., 2011). Understanding the metallothionein gene will help to unveil a new horizon for salt tolerance and will reduce the distance between halophyte and glycophyte. The gene transformation of OcMT3 gene to species of Oryza sativa (BRRI Dhan 67) has been done but the functional genomics and exploration of O. coarctata metallothionein gene is still underexplored (Seraj et al., 2023).

**Title:** OcMT3 Metallothionein from Wild Rice *Oryza coarctata*: A Candidate Gene for Salt Tolerance in Cultivated Rice

**Background**
Salinity threatens rice production in South Asia (Dasgupta et al., 2018). *O. sativa* is a salt-sensitive glycophyte (Hoang et al., 2016), while *O. coarctata* is a halophyte tolerating up to 240 mM NaCl (Prusty et al., 2026) and >400 mM (Ishikawa et al., 2022). *O. sativa* is higher yielding but lacks this tolerance. Metallothionein is a key regulator in *O. coarctata* salt tolerance (Kumar et al., 2012). OcMT3 shares 82% homology with OsMT-I-3a (Usha et al., 2011). OcMT3 transformation into BRRI Dhan 67 has been done, but functional differences between OcMT3 and OsMT remain underexplored (Tasnim et al., 2023).
