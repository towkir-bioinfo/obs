## Lecture 1: Proteomics: Area and Scope

### Definition

The word **"proteome"** represents the complete protein pool of an organism encoded by the genome.

In broader terms, **Proteomics** is defined as the total protein content of a cell or that of an organism.

### Key Applications

- Understanding alteration in protein expression during different stages of life cycle or under stress condition
- Understanding the structure and function of different proteins as well as protein-protein interactions of an organism
- Detection of minor defects in protein structure, function, or expression patterns

### Significance in Drug Development

- Proteins are the most favorable targets for various drugs
- The study of the proteome is crucial for:
  - Identifying novel therapeutic targets
  - Understanding disease mechanisms
  - Assessing drug efficacy and toxicity
- Proteomic techniques, notably mass spectrometry, analyze protein expression, post-translational modifications, and interactions to optimize drug discovery

---

## Key Applications in Drug Development

### 1. Target Identification and Validation
- Identifies proteins dysregulated in disease, serving as potential drug targets
- Provides a 3D map of the cell to locate these targets

### 2. Mechanism of Action (MoA)
- Uses proteome dynamics profiling to observe how drugs alter protein stability, production, and signalling pathways

### 3. Drug-Protein Interactions
- Techniques like affinity purification and thermal proteome profiling identify binding sites and affinities
- Helps understand off-target effects

### 4. Biomarker Discovery and Patient Stratification
- Aids in identifying biomarkers for early diagnosis
- Monitors disease progression
- Selects patients for clinical trials to improve success rates

### 5. Safety and Toxicity Assessment
- Predicts potential side effects and toxicity early in development

## Examples of Drug Development Based on Proteomic Studies

| Drug/Area | Technology | Proteomic Insight |
|-----------|------------|-------------------|
| Trastuzumab (Cancer) | Profiling | Identified HER2 protein overexpression |
| Gilteritinib (AML) | Phosphoproteomics | Identified activated kinase signaling (FLT3) |
| Palbociclib (Myeloma) | TMT-Quantitative | Identified CDK6 activation in resistant patients |
| Repurposing (Various) | TPP/Chemical | Identified protein targets for existing drugs |

### Example 1: Targeted Cancer Therapy (HER2 in Breast Cancer)
- Proteomic profiling identified HER2 protein overexpression in certain aggressive breast cancer subtypes
- Result: Development of **Trastuzumab (Herceptin)**, a monoclonal antibody designed to bind and inhibit HER2

### Example 2: Identifying New Targets in Myeloid Leukemia (AML)
- Phosphoproteomics used to compare AML patient samples (FLT3-WT vs. FLT3-ITD)
- Result: FLT3-ITD mutations lead to activation of specific downstream signaling pathways
- Led to use of **Gilteritinib** and **Midostaurin** to target FLT3 protein
- Suggested combining MEK inhibitor (trametinib) with FLT3 inhibitor for improved outcomes

### Example 3: Drug Repurposing via Thermal Proteome Profiling (TPP)
- TPP measures changes in protein stability at different temperatures
- Example: Topotecan binds to several DNA replication and metabolism proteins, not just Topoisomerase 1
- Repurposing: Progesterone identified for treating certain cancers by targeting receptor ADGRD1

### Example 4: Overcoming Drug Resistance in Multiple Myeloma
- Quantitative proteomics (TMT-labeled) studied relapsed patients resistant to lenalidomide
- Result: CDK6 highly activated in relapsed samples
- Strategy: Combining lenalidomide with CDK6 inhibitor (palbociclib)

### Example 5: Alzheimer's Disease Biomarker and Target Discovery
- Proteomic analysis identified proteins associated with amyloid plaque formation and neurodegeneration
- Result: Used to develop drugs targeting plaque buildup and early diagnostic blood-based biomarkers

## TMT-Quantitative/LC-MS/MS Analysis

- **Tandem Mass Tag (TMT)** is a high-throughput, isobaric labeling technology for accurate, multiplexed quantitative proteomics
- Allows pooling up to 16-18 samples in a single LC-MS/MS run
- Increases throughput, reduces missing values, enables precise quantification
- Ideal for biomarker discovery, disease mechanisms, and PTM studies

## Lecture 3: Proteome Complexity and Imbalance

### Proteome Complexity
- The cellular proteome is a complex microcosm of structural and regulatory networks
- Requires continuous surveillance and modification to meet dynamic needs of the cell
- Protein flux must remain in balance for proper cell function

### Causes of Proteome Imbalance
- Genetic alterations (from chromosome imbalance to oncogene activation)
- Increased protein production demands
- Disruptions in protein biogenesis and degradation systems

### Consequences
- Functional defects
- Cell death
- Development of diseases like cancer and neurodegenerative disorders

## Lecture 4: Areas of Proteomics Study

Proteomics can be broadly divided into three main areas:

### 1. Expression Proteomics
Studies changes in protein expression, both qualitatively and quantitatively, under different conditions:
- Comparing healthy and diseased states
- Different cell types
- Developmental stages

#### Key Techniques:
- **Mass Spectrometry**: Identifies and quantifies proteins based on mass-to-charge ratio
- **Two-Dimensional Gel Electrophoresis (2DGE)**: Separates proteins based on size and charge
- **Antibody-Based Assays**: ELISA and Western Blotting
- **Microarrays**: Measure expression levels and binding affinities
- **Liquid Chromatography (LC)**: Protein separation
- **Shotgun Proteomics**: Bottom-up approach using HPLC and mass spectrometry

#### Applications:
- Disease diagnosis and biomarker discovery
- Drug discovery and target identification
- Understanding disease mechanisms
- Comparative analysis between different samples or conditions

## Lecture 5: PEA Technology and Biomarkers

### Proximity Extension Assay (PEA)
- Method for detecting and quantifying many specific proteins in biological samples (serum, plasma)
- Used in affinity proteomics for biomarker discovery

### Key Biomarkers by Disease Category

**Cancer (Oncology):**
- Prostate: PSA
- Ovarian/Pancreatic: CA125, CA19-9
- Breast/Ovarian: BRCA1 and BRCA2 mutations
- Lung/AML: Gene fusions and mutation analysis

**Cardiovascular:**
- Heart Attack: Cardiac troponins
- Heart Disease Risk: Cholesterol levels

**Neurological (Dementia):**
- Alzheimer's: Amyloid-beta, tau proteins, APOE ε4 allele

**Inflammatory & Digestive:**
- IBD: Fecal calprotectin

**Metabolic & Genetic:**
- Cystic Fibrosis/Sickle Cell: Genetic markers
- Diabetes: Hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c)

### Types of Biomarkers
- **Diagnostic**: Detects current disease
- **Prognostic**: Predicts course/outcome
- **Predictive**: Assesses treatment response
- **Susceptibility/Risk**: Indicates future disease potential

### Olink PEA Technology
- Combines antibody specificity with PCR sensitivity and amplification
- Uses two proximity probes with antibody linked to unique DNA oligonucleotide
- When probes bind target protein, DNA oligonucleotides hybridize
- Unique DNA sequence generated, quantifiable via real-time PCR or NGS
- NGS counts proportional to protein concentration

## Research Application: Multiple Sclerosis Proteomics

**Study**: Proteomics reveal biomarkers for diagnosis, disease activity and long-term disability outcomes in multiple sclerosis

**Methods**:
- Used Olink Explore platform (PEA technology with NGS readout)
- Quantified 1,463 proteins in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and plasma
- 143 people with early-stage MS and 43 healthy controls

**Key Findings**:
- Lower levels of neurofilament light chain (NfL) in CSF predicts absence of disease activity
- Identified 11 CSF proteins (CXCL13, LTA, FCN2, ICAM3, LY9, SLAMF7, TYMP, CHI3L1, FYB1, TNFRSF1B, NfL) predicting severity of disability worsening

## Lecture 6: Structural Proteomics

### Definition
Structural proteomics encompasses the large-scale determination of protein structures and their interactions, offering insights into protein function, drug discovery, and disease mechanisms.

### Key Techniques

1. **Mass Spectrometry (MS)** : Protein sequencing, PTM identification, protein-protein interactions
2. **X-ray Crystallography**: 3D structure determination by X-ray diffraction
3. **Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) Spectroscopy**: Structures in solution, dynamics, interactions
4. **Cryo-Electron Microscopy (cryo-EM)** : Near-atomic resolution for large protein complexes
5. **Chemical Labeling and Cross-linking**: Probe structures and interactions
6. **Limited Proteolysis (LiP)** : Study protein conformation and drug binding sites

### Proteomics Workflow

1. Sample collection
2. Lysis
3. Protein purification
4. Protein fractionation
5. LC-MS/MS
6. Protein identification

### Characterization Methods

| Technique | Application |
|-----------|-------------|
| ELISA | Quantification |
| Western blotting | Detection |
| Protein microarray | High-throughput screening |
| Mass spectrometry | Identification |
| Edman sequencing | Protein sequencing |
| ICAT/SILAC/iTRAQ | Quantitative proteomics |
| X-ray crystallography | 3D structure |
| NMR spectroscopy | Solution structure |

## Lecture 7: Proteomics Methods and Steps

### 1. Sample Preparation
- Critical step involving extraction and purification of proteins
- Uses organic solvents and detergents for solubility and membrane disruption
- Mechanical homogenization or sonication for tissue disruption

### 2. Separation and Isolation of Proteins

#### Gel-Based Approaches:

**1-DE (SDS-PAGE)** :
- Separates proteins based on molecular weight
- SDS denatures proteins and imparts uniform negative charge
- Smaller proteins move faster through polyacrylamide gel

**2-DE** :
- First dimension: Isoelectric focusing (IEF) - separates by isoelectric point (pI)
- Second dimension: SDS-PAGE - separates by molecular weight
- Better differentiation, detects PTMs and protein isoforms
- Limitations: Cannot detect very low or high molecular weight proteins

#### Chromatography-Based Approaches:
- **IEC (Ion Exchange Chromatography)** : Based on charge
- **SEC (Size Exclusion Chromatography)** : Based on size
- **Affinity Chromatography**: Based on interaction with immobilized ligand
- **LC (Liquid Chromatography)** : Widely used for separating proteins from complex mixtures

### 3. Protein Identification and Characterization

#### Mass Spectrometry (MS):
- Rapidly sequences proteins and determines molecular weights
- Ionization methods:
  - **ESI (Electrospray Ionization)** : Creates charged droplets, increases gaseous ion production
  - **MALDI (Matrix-Assisted Laser Desorption Ionization)** : Uses chemical matrix, transfers energy to sample

#### Database Searching:
- Bioinformatics programs search for protein identity in known databases
- User provides: molecular masses of peptide, peptide sequence, molecular weight, isoelectric point
- Limited to model organisms with sequenced genomes

## Lecture 8: 2-D Gel Electrophoresis

### Principles
- Two-dimensional gel electrophoresis (2DE) separates proteins based on:
  1. **Isoelectric point (pI)** - first dimension
  2. **Molecular weight (MW)** - second dimension

### Procedure

1. **Sample Preparation**: Proteins extracted, solubilized in denaturing buffer
2. **First Dimension (IEF)** : Electric field applied across pH gradient strip; proteins focus at their pI
3. **Second Dimension (SDS-PAGE)** : IEF strip placed on SDS-PAGE gel; proteins separated by size
4. **Visualization**: Staining (Coomassie blue, silver stain, fluorescent dyes)

### Applications
- Differential protein expression analysis
- Protein identification (excise spots → digest → MS)
- PTM analysis (phosphorylation, glycosylation)
- Biomarker discovery
- Protein interaction studies

### Advantages
- High resolution (thousands of proteins per gel)
- Visual representation
- Versatile applications

### Limitations
- Time-consuming and labor-intensive
- Limited sensitivity
- Reproducibility issues
- Not ideal for membrane proteins

### Modern Alternatives
- 2D DIGE (Difference Gel Electrophoresis): Improved reproducibility, labels different samples with fluorescent dyes

## Lecture: Functional Proteomics

### Definition
- Focused on understanding the functions of proteins and their interactions with other molecules in the cell
- Identifies protein complexes and interactions involved in specific biological processes
- Determines roles of individual proteins within these complexes

### Protein Arrays
- Genes are arrayed on a surface with transcription and translation in situ
- Various formats:
  - DNA array to protein array (DAPA)
  - Nucleic acid programmable protein array (NAPPA)
  - Multiple in situ spotting technique (MIST)
  - In situ puromycin capture from mRNA arrays

### Example: Hemophilia
- Rare disorder where blood doesn't clot properly
- Caused by insufficient blood-clotting proteins (clotting factors)
