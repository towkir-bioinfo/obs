# Last universal common ancestor - LUCA
**[[LUCA]] = Last Universal Common Ancestor** — the most recent organism from which every living thing on Earth today descends: all Bacteria, Archaea, and Eukaryotes.

**Three things people commonly get wrong:**

1. **It's not the first life.** Life existed before LUCA. LUCA is just the most recent ancestor whose descendants *all survived to today* — earlier and sister lineages went extinct, leaving no living representatives.
2. **It's probably not one individual.** More likely a whole population or community exchanging genes freely, which is why "ancestor" is a bit misleading.
3. **It wasn't simple.** Reconstructions suggest it was already a fairly sophisticated cell.

**What it likely had** (inferred from features universal across all three domains):
- A DNA genome, essentially the modern genetic code, and ribosomes
- ATP as energy currency, a lipid membrane
- Weiss et al. (2016) inferred ~355 gene families; a 2024 *Nature Ecology & Evolution* study (Moody et al.) argued for a much larger genome (~2,600 proteins) and even an anti-viral defence system
- Probably anaerobic and thermophilic — hydrothermal-vent-like conditions, though this part is genuinely contested
- Dating estimates range ~3.5–4.2 billion years ago

**Why it's in a central dogma lecture** — this is the connection your note is building toward:

The DNA → RNA → protein machinery is *universal*. Every organism, from *E. coli* to rice to humans, uses the same genetic code, the same ribosomes, the same polymerases. The most parsimonious explanation is that this system was **already running in LUCA** and has been inherited ever since, rather than being invented independently many times. The central dogma is essentially LUCA's legacy — that's why it's "central" and why it's the same in your *O. coarctata* work as in the neuroblastoma atlas papers.
