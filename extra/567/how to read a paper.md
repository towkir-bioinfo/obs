# How to read a paper

Phase-by-phase reading strategy: which section to read for which question, and
the signal words that mark each one.

## Title

`Study type + Data type + Key findings = good title`

## Phase 1 — Abstract

**Question it answers:** what is this paper about?

**Structured abstract** — Background, Methods, Results, Conclusion.

| Section | What it contains | Signal words |
|---|---|---|
| Background | Introduction of the topic + justification (why) + aim/goal/purpose | while, however |
| Methods | What researchers did (simple past tense) | investigated, conducted, employed, performed |
| Results | What they found | revealed, found, showed |
| Conclusion | Key findings + future direction / recommendation | key findings, major findings, concluded, in summary, needs, suggests |

**Unstructured abstract** — there are no sections; look for the keywords instead.

## Phase 2 — Objectives, hypothesis, research questions

**Read:** the last paragraph of the Introduction.

**Look for:** aim, goal, purpose, hypothesis.

## Phase 3 — Key findings

**Read:** ~~last~~ first paragraph of the Discussion.

**Look for:** key findings, major results/findings.

## Phase 4 — Limitations

**Read:** the last paragraph of the Discussion.

**Look for:** limitations.

## Phase 5 — Key findings and future directions

**Read:** the Conclusion.

**Look for:** key findings / main findings / major findings; future direction,
suggests, recommended, future action plans.

## Phase 6 — Methods and results

**Read:** Methods + Results.

Statistical method + why (purpose).

> We conducted logistic regression to assess the relationship between hearth
> diseases and excoriates.

**Look for:** conducted, investigated, employed, performed — these introduce the
statistical methods.

## Paragraph structure

Each paragraph typically runs:

1. **Topic sentence** — introduces the topic
2. **Supporting info**
3. **Concluding sentence**
